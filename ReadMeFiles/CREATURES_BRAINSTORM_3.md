## Tenth roster pass — the hundredth species

Part 3 opens where the count demanded: two entries from
the ends of the Size spectrum, saved to stand together —
the largest creature on the roster, and the one that was
always waiting at the player's own door.

**99. Grass Leviathan** — the far plains (Meadow x Storm
country and the deep Meadow beyond it; affinity recorded
for both). Fauna x Ordinary. Shape: Quadruped; modifiers:
hill-backed — its back grows MEADOW, a walking acre of
grass, clover, and whatever has rooted in it (Tumble Sages
anchor on it seasonally; meadow hoppers winter in its
grass; it is the roster's first WALKABLE creature, and the
design records that plainly: you can stand on it, and it
does not mind, and it does not notice). Locomotion: Walking
— its stride period is minutes; its migration is a season;
from the ground it reads as weather arriving (the grass
bends ahead of it for a mile, and herds travel in its wake
eating what its passage uncovered). Spectra: abyssally
calm / SOLO, with an entourage it never invited / Speed:
a horizon's / Size: the spectrum's top anchor — beyond
LARGE there is only this (the template records it as
LANDSCAPE, and the design accepts the new word once) /
Diet grazer of whole meadows, in passing / Day-night: it
sleeps standing, mid-stride, for days; the plains' caravans
time their camps to its naps / Curiosity none — at its
scale, a player is a rumor the grass tells / Territory a
migration, walked for centuries / UNTAMABLE — you cannot
tame geography; you can FOLLOW it, and generations of the
plains' creatures do (a leviathan-route is the far
country's oldest road: cleared, grazed, and renewed by
the walking itself). Drops: none taken; its shed winter
coat-slabs at the migration's end are meadow in brick
form, and the codex leaves them as scenery. Hook: every
roster of wonders needs one citizen that resets the
player's sense of size — the Bonefields keep a dead one
as a house; the plains keep a live one as a ROAD.

**100. Hearth Dog** — everywhere a player settles
(ubiquitous layer; the layer's fourth designed member).
Fauna x Ordinary. Shape: Quadruped (dog plan, mid-sized,
unremarkable in every proportion — deliberately: after
ninety-nine wonders, the hundredth is a DOG). Locomotion:
Walking. Its law, and the design states it as the species'
whole template entry in one breath: a hearth dog is not
found, caught, or tamed — it ARRIVES. When a base crosses
the line from camp to home (a hearth kept, a roof kept, a
garden kept — the game's own measures, numbers deferred),
a dog comes out of the country one morning and takes up
at the doorstep, the way the Hearth Wisp takes up at a
healthy fire: the wisp certifies the fire; the dog
certifies the HOME. Spectra: loyal, self-possessed /
Social: one per household, courteous to its kind / Speed:
a dog's / Size mid / Diet the household's leavings, by
right and by arrangement / Day-night: the household's /
Curiosity total, domestic / Territory the doorstep, the
yard, the walk to the fields — a perimeter it invents and
then keeps / Tameability: REVERSED — the template's one
inversion, recorded proudly: the bonding runs the other
way. Feed it three days, keep its hours, and it decides;
a decided dog is the roster's most complete pet (follow,
stay, and guard all at full fluency — it has been watching
how you live, and has views) — and a dog that has NOT
decided will leave one morning as it came, and the design
lets it, because a hundredth species should be allowed to
teach the roster's deepest rule by example: in BioForge,
even domestication is a relationship, not a mechanic.
Drops: none. It is not that kind of citizen. Hook: the
roster designed the world's edges first — its emblems,
its weathers, its dead gods' gardening staff — and saved
the doorstep for #100 on purpose. This is the animal
every player will know longest.

### Hybrid wave four

**101. Root Otter** — Wetland x Rootwood (place name
draft: the Drowned Roots). Fauna x Ordinary. Shape:
Quadruped; modifiers: sleek, long, unbothered by either
parent's element. Locomotion: Swimming primary + Climbing
secondary — it treats flooded root country as one
continuous medium (water, root, slide, water) and is the
hybrid's proof that the two families were always one
country waiting for its citizen. Spectra: playful,
professionally / small family rafts / quick / mid / Diet
hunter-picker of pool and root alike / diurnal / Curiosity
TOTAL — an otter investigates a player the way the Storm
Crow does, at closer range and with wetter hands: it
STEALS small bright things into root-caches (the crow's
barter economy has a wet branch; what the otter considers
payment is a logic of its own, and returned items appear
in caches like dividends) / Territory a root-and-pool
circuit / Tameability 40% — by playing along, over days.
Drops: its caches themselves — found caches hold **Otter

category, not an item; the generator tables decide the
treasure, the otter decides the bank. Hook: the hybrid
casts needed a citizen whose whole body is an argument
that the border isn't real.

**102. Logback** — Fungal x Rootwood (place name draft:

with less paperwork). Drops: its trail, gathered, is
premium compost-grade — unlisted, per the house
discipline for honest small change. Hook: the Gloom Slug
eats darkness in the Underroot; the logback eats the
FOREST ITSELF in the Rotwood — the family's two slugs are
the two halves of its recycling, above the roots and
below them.

— they bond to a NEST-SITE, not a person (the Spire Bat's

camouflage as a family resemblance.

**105. Relay Wisp** — Precursor x Storm (the far margin).

roster's lighthouse — a citizen whose whole use is that

## Eleventh roster pass — the semi-ubiquitous foundation

Adam's layer, founded (the amendment in Part 1 carries
the definition): twelve wide-range species spanning the
six ranges, so every family's stack thickens at once.
Coverage, stated honestly in the pass rather than
discovered later: the temperate middle gains thickly
(Meadow-family zones now carry up to nine of the twelve),
the cold and far edges thinly (Frost and Ember two each,
the Living and Precursor one each) — because cold, fire,
and weirdness are FILTERS, and the layer's honesty is
Adam's whole point (mice don't live in the arctic).
Wave two of the layer is already named by its gaps:
the Far Bat (Storm / Precursor / Living / deep Fungal)
first, then cold- and warm-range thickeners.

**111. Hedge Sparrow** — Temperate + Wet ranges
(Meadow, Rootwood, Fungal, Wetland). Fauna x Ordinary.
Shape: Winged (sparrow plan). Locomotion: Aerial,
flyer-leaning + Hopping. The everywhere-bird of the
settled graph: hedges, root eaves, marsh banks, garden
walls. Spectra: bold and cheerful / loose colonies /
quick / small / Diet seeds and small insects / dawn —
its territory songs are how the settled country wakes,

traveler hears the border before the label names it) /
Curiosity high, crumb-led / Territory a hedge and its

homestead's second tenant. Behavior worth its data:
sparrows mob-alarm at predators in a rising storm of
noise — a sparrow-storm overhead is the temperate
country's siren, and the design records it as the
species' real drop: INFORMATION. Hook: the baseline
bird; the roster's familiar note, sung at dawn in four
accents.
