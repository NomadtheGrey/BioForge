## Seventeenth roster pass — the epic blanks

Adam's standing order (2026-10-15): fill in the blanks
for epic-large creatures at will. This pass completes
the epic set — every family and medium that lacked a
landscape-class citizen gets one, plus the HUGE tier
beneath them where a family had no giant at all.

**The epic distribution ruling** (Adam asked: noise-
generated, random, or what? — answered here, once, for
all epics, retroactive to the Leviathans and the
Whale): epics are NOT census and NOT noise scatter.
An epic is a UNIQUE INDIVIDUAL, seeded like geography:
exactly one per epic species per world (per far
region, for the migratory ones), placed by the world
seed deterministically — same seed, same giant, same
migration — the way zone rolls and ruin fates are
seeded, not the way herds scatter. Noise decides where
the herds are; the seed itself decides the giants,
once. An epic does not respawn: if one dies, the
world is permanently poorer, and the codex records
the date. They are discovered by SIGN before they are
ever seen — tracks, shed weather, the entourage's
behavior — because meeting one should feel like
finding a mountain that wasn't on the map, and the
map was right about everything else.

## Nineteenth roster pass — the alien rivers

The biome program's open list asked for this pass by
name: "Rivers flowing with weird liquids rather than
water are wanted but undesigned: courses and banks,
what the liquid does (flow, buoyancy, hazard,
resource), and which creatures live in and around
it." Courses and banks belong to the world-builders;
the liquid dossiers and the citizens are this chat's
slice, designed here. Three river systems (the fourth
liquid, the Fens' scalding water, is ordinary water
with the temperature turned up, and needs no dossier).

**DOSSIER — QUICKSILVER (the Silver Rivers; far
Storm/Ember-magnetic country).** Flow: fast, heavy,
silent — it does not splash; it POURS, and its banks
are polished. Buoyancy: EXTREME — dense enough that
wood, stone-lightened gear, and small animals float
HIGH; a person floats like a cork and cannot dive
(wading is walking ON a giving floor). Hazard: the
COATING — it films skin and gear, heavy and slick,
worn off slowly; nothing drinks it and lives
unchanged. Resource: it CONDUCTS — storm country
runs charge along silver rivers like wires, and the
flats' peoples read a river's shimmer for the
weather's intentions.

**DOSSIER — AMBER (the Sap Rivers; Rootwood x Living
country, the Speaking Wood's low ground).** Flow:
glacial — a river you can outwalk at a stroll, with
a current measured in days. Buoyancy: NEGATIVE and
slow — things sink at a sinking's leisure and are
PRESERVED mid-sink, for centuries, at whatever depth
they reached (the banks are museums: mid-swim fish,
mid-fall leaves, someone's dropped tool, all held).
Hazard: entrapment by patience — step in carelessly
and the river keeps the boot, then negotiates.
Resource: the preserved world itself, and Heart
Sap's strong kin.

**DOSSIER — STILLWATER (the Mirror Rivers; the
Mirror country's deep form).** Flow: imperceptible —
a black glass moving at a rumor's pace. Buoyancy:
ordinary, with one wrongness: REFLECTIONS LAG — what
the surface shows is a half-second old, everywhere,
always (the country's founding rule, extended from
marsh to river: in Mirror country, the present is
only available directly). Hazard: navigation and
nerve — nothing in the reflection can be trusted to
be where it is. Resource: the stillest water in the
world, for the far crafts that need a perfect
surface.
